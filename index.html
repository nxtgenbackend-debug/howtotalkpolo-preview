By Don Lubner, polo announcer

First polo match? Read this book.

Don Lubner served as announcer for the Milwaukee Polo Club for many years, learning to love polo from the fan’s perspective. His book, How To Talk Polo: A Fan’s Perspective, is designed as a primer for new fans to learn the etiquette and rules of horse polo.

The book

How to Talk Polo: A Fan’s Perspective

Written for the sideline, not the saddle

You don’t need to ride horses to enjoy polo. Most polo books are written to help polo players improve their game or decorate your coffee table. How To Talk Polo is designed exclusively for the fans to be a concise yet humorous guide to a sport many Americans have worn or heard of, but never watched.

Six words to start with

Take the polo quiz →
chukkernoun
One period of play, about seven minutes long. A match runs four to six of them.
ponynoun
Every polo horse is called a pony, however tall it is.
handicapnoun
A player’s rating, from −2 to 10 goals. It measures skill, not how many goals they score.
line of the ballnoun
The path the ball is travelling. The rider following it has the right of way.
ride-offnoun
Two riders pressing shoulder to shoulder, each pushing the other off the line.
hookverb
To block an opponent’s swing with your own mallet.
