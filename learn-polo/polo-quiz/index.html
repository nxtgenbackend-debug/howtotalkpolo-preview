Polo pop quiz: how well do you talk polo?

Ten questions about the game, from chukkers to the divot stomp. Pick an answer and you’ll see straight away whether you’re right, and why.

  1. 1How long is one chukker?

    Show the answer

    About seven minutes. A chukker is one period of play, about seven minutes. Club matches usually play four, and the top level plays six. chukker in the glossary.

  2. 2What is every polo horse called, however tall it is?

    Show the answer

    A pony. Every polo horse is a pony. Most are Thoroughbreds or Thoroughbred crosses, and a player brings several to each match. pony in the glossary.

  3. 3How many players does each team have on a grass field?

    Show the answer

    Four. Four a side, numbered 1 to 4. Arena polo, played in a smaller walled ring, has three. Number 1 in the glossary.

  4. 4What does a player's handicap measure?

    Show the answer

    Their skill, rated from −2 to 10 goals. The handicap is a skill rating set by the national association. A team's handicap is the four players' handicaps added together. handicap in the glossary.

  5. 5Which hand must every player hold the mallet in?

    Show the answer

    The right hand. All players must play right-handed, so every mallet is on the off side, the right side of the pony. off side in the glossary.

  6. 6What happens after every goal?

    Show the answer

    The teams change ends. Teams change ends after every goal, so neither side keeps the wind or the better ground for long. goal in the glossary.

  7. 7What is the divot stomp?

    Show the answer

    Spectators treading the torn-up turf back into place at halftime. At halftime the crowd walks onto the field to press the divots back down. It's the one moment spectators get to play their part. divot stomp in the glossary.

  8. 8The rider following the line of the ball has what?

    Show the answer

    The right of way. The line of the ball is the path the ball travels. The rider following it has the right of way, and crossing in front of them is a foul. line of the ball in the glossary.

  9. 9Which player is the team's back, guarding its own goal?

    Show the answer

    Number 4. The Number 4 is the back, the last line of defence, who sends the ball forward to the Number 3. Number 4 in the glossary.

  10. 10How far apart are the goal posts?

    Show the answer

    Eight yards. The posts stand eight yards apart, and there is no crossbar, so it counts as a goal at any height. goal in the glossary.
