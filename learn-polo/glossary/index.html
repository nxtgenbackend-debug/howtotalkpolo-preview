Polo terms and definitions, A to Z

Polo has its own language, and that’s the whole idea of the book. Start with the question most newcomers ask: polo horses are called ponies, however tall they are. Here are all 43 polo terms, so the next thing the announcer says makes sense.

Asset #10These definitions are ours for now. With the family’s permission, each one gets Don’s own wording from the book.
ABCDFGHKLMNOPRSTU

A

appealnoun
A player raising a mallet, or a hand, to ask the umpires for a foul. The umpires decide; the appeal itself counts for nothing. See umpire, foul.
arena polonoun
Polo played in a smaller walled arena, three players a side, with a larger inflated ball. Common in winter and at colleges.

B

backnoun
The Number 4, the team's last line of defence, who usually plays nearest their own goal. See Number 4.
backshotnoun also backhand
A shot that sends the ball the opposite way to the one the pony is travelling. Often the quickest way to turn the play around.
bellnoun
The signal that seven minutes of a chukker are up. Play usually runs on until the ball goes out or another thirty seconds pass, except in the last chukker, which ends at the bell. See chukker.
boardsplural noun
The low wooden boards along the sidelines of many fields, which keep the ball in play. See field.
bumpnoun
Riding into an opponent's pony at a safe angle to push it off the line. Allowed; riding in at a dangerous angle is not. See ride-off, line of the ball.

C

chukkernoun also chukka
One period of play, about seven minutes long. A match runs four to six of them, and riders change ponies between them. See bell, pony.
crossverb
To ride across the line of the ball in front of a player who has the right of way. The foul at the heart of polo's rules, because at a gallop it is the most dangerous. See line of the ball, right of way, foul.

D

divot stompnoun
The halftime tradition of spectators walking onto the field to tread the torn-up turf back into place. See halftime.

F

fieldnoun
The playing ground: up to 300 yards long and 160 wide if boarded, about nine American football fields. The largest field in organised sport. See boards, goal.
forehandnoun
A shot hit forward, in the direction the pony is travelling. See backshot, near side, off side.
foulnoun
A breach of the rules, almost always about safety: crossing, dangerous riding or a dangerous swing of the mallet. Fouls are punished with penalties. See cross, penalty.

G

goalnoun
A point scored by hitting the ball between the opponents' posts, which stand eight yards apart. There is no crossbar, so height doesn't matter. After every goal the teams change ends.
groomnoun
The person who cares for a player's ponies, readies them for each chukker and keeps the spares on the pony line. See pony line.

H

halftimenoun
The break in the middle of a match, longer than the gaps between chukkers, and the time for the divot stomp. See divot stomp.
handicapnoun
A player's rating, from −2 to 10 goals, set by the national association. It measures skill, not goals scored. A team's handicap is the sum of its four players'. See high-goal.
high-goaladjective
Describes the top level of the sport, played by teams with the highest combined handicaps. Medium-goal and low-goal polo follow, at lower totals. See handicap.
hookverb
To block an opponent's swing by catching their mallet with your own. Allowed only when you are on the same side as the swing, or directly behind it. See mallet.
Hurlinghamnoun
The Hurlingham Polo Association, the governing body of polo in Britain, named after the London club where the modern rules were written. See USPA.

K

knock-innoun
The restart when the attacking side sends the ball over the back line without scoring: the defenders hit it back into play from their own back line. See safety.

L

line of the ballnoun
The path the ball travels, extended forward and back. The rider following it has the right of way, and most of the rules exist to protect that. See right of way, cross.

M

malletnoun also stick
A cane shaft, roughly 48 to 54 inches long, with a cigar-shaped head. The ball is struck with the long side of the head, not the end.

N

near sidenoun
The left side of the pony. A near-side shot is hit across the pony's body on that side. See off side.
neck shotnoun
A shot hit under the pony's neck, from one side to the other. See tail shot.
Number 1noun
The forward and main attacker, who lives near the opponents' goal and marks their Number 4. See Number 4.
Number 2noun
The workhorse of the team, attacking and defending in turn, and the player most often in a ride-off.
Number 3noun
Usually the strongest player on the team: the playmaker, who turns defence into attack from the middle of the field.
Number 4noun
The back, the defender, who guards the goal and sends the ball forward to the Number 3. See back.

O

off sidenoun
The right side of the pony, the side every mallet is held on, since all players must swing right-handed. See near side.

P

penaltynoun
The free hit awarded after a foul, taken from 30, 40 or 60 yards out or from where the foul happened, depending on how serious it was. The most serious fouls award a goal outright. See foul.
ponynoun
Every polo horse is a pony, however tall it is. Most are Thoroughbreds or Thoroughbred crosses, and a player brings several to each match. See chukker, pony line.
pony linenoun
The row of ponies, held by grooms beside the field, waiting for their next chukker. See groom.

R

refereenoun also third man
The official on the sideline who settles any disagreement between the two mounted umpires. See umpire.
ride-offnoun
Two riders pressing shoulder to shoulder, each trying to push the other off the line of the ball. See bump, line of the ball.
right of waynoun
The right to ride along the line of the ball, which belongs to the player following it. No one may cross in front of them. See line of the ball, cross.

S

safetynoun
The free hit from 60 yards given to the attackers when a defender sends the ball over their own back line. See knock-in.
stick and ballnoun
Practice: a player alone on the field, hitting balls to school a pony or sharpen a swing.
sudden deathnoun
The extra chukker played when a match ends level. The first goal wins it. See chukker.

T

tail shotnoun
A shot hit behind the pony, under or past its tail. See neck shot.
throw-innoun
How play starts and restarts: an umpire bowls the ball between the two teams lined up facing each other. See umpire.

U

umpirenoun
One of the two officials on horseback who ride with the play and call the fouls. See referee, appeal.
USPAnoun
The United States Polo Association, founded in 1890, the sport's governing body in America. It sets the rules and the players' handicaps. See handicap, Hurlingham.
