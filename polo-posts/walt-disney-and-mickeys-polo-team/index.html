Walt Disney and Mickey's Polo Team

By Charles Lubner · Oct 14, 2026

How the Sport of Kings Captivated Walt Disney

Most people know Walt Disney as the creator of Mickey Mouse and one of the great pioneers of American animation. Far fewer know that Walt Disney was also an enthusiastic polo player.

During the 1930s, Disney became captivated by polo at a time when the sport was enormously popular among Hollywood actors, filmmakers and celebrities. What began as a recreational escape from the pressures of his growing studio developed into a serious passion—and eventually found its way into Disney animation.

Why Did Walt Disney Start Playing Polo?

Walt Disney lived and worked under considerable pressure as his animation studio expanded. Looking for a physical activity that could provide a break from work, he experimented with several sports before becoming increasingly interested in horseback riding.

That interest eventually led him to polo.

The combination made sense. Polo offered horses, competition, exercise and camaraderie while providing Disney with an opportunity to escape the demands of the studio for an afternoon.He soon became fascinated with the game.

Polo and Hollywood in the 1930s

To understand Disney’s enthusiasm, it helps to understand just how fashionable polo was in Southern California during the 1930s.

Polo wasn’t simply a sporting event. Matches attracted actors, producers, studio executives and other members of Hollywood society. The polo field became another place where the entertainment community could gather away from the movie studios.

Among the celebrities most closely associated with the sport was Will Rogers, one of America’s most famous entertainers and an accomplished horseman. Rogers was an enthusiastic promoter of polo and became part of the Hollywood polo culture that attracted Disney to the game. For Disney, the timing was perfect. His growing interest in horses and his position within Hollywood made polo a natural fit.

Walt Disney Learned to Play Polo Seriously

Disney didn’t merely attend matches and occasionally swing a mallet. He wanted to learn how to play the game properly. Along with his brother Roy and friends and colleagues, Disney pursued polo instruction and studied the sport away from the field as well.

One of the books associated with his study of polo was William Cameron Forbes’ As to Polo, an early guide to playing and understanding the game. Disney’s enthusiasm even extended to practicing his swing when he couldn’t get onto a horse.

Special practice devices sometimes called polo “cages” allowed riders to sit on stationary wooden horses and practice hitting polo balls. Disney reportedly had practice equipment available at both his studio and home. Polo had clearly become much more than a casual pastime.

On fame, “It feels good when it helps to get a good seat for a football game. But it never helped me make a good film or a good shot in a polo game, or command the obedience of my daughter.”

Walt Disney, American entrepreneur, animator, voice actor and film producer.

Walt Disney’s Polo Team

Disney eventually began playing competitively with friends and associates. His brother Roy Disney also became involved in polo, and Walt played alongside other members of Hollywood’s growing polo community. Disney and several companions even formed a team that became affectionately associated with the name Mickey Mouse.

For Walt, the appeal of polo appears to have gone beyond simply winning matches. On the polo field, deadlines, drawings, budgets and production problems were temporarily replaced by horses, teammates, opponents, mallets and a small white ball. It was an entirely different world from the animation studio.

Will Rogers, Walt Disney and Polo

One of the most important personalities connecting Hollywood and polo during this period was Will Rogers.

Rogers was a hugely popular humorist, actor and cowboy philosopher as well as an accomplished horseman. His enthusiasm helped make polo fashionable among members of Hollywood’s entertainment community during the 1920s and 1930s. Disney’s growing interest in polo developed within this same Hollywood sporting culture.

Their connection would almost be immortalized in animation. When Disney began developing an animated polo short, Rogers was reportedly intended to appear as one of the Hollywood celebrities caricatured in the film. Disney artists even studied and sketched Rogers in preparation for his animated appearance. Tragically, Rogers never saw the finished cartoon.

On August 15, 1935, Will Rogers and famed aviator Wiley Post were killed in an airplane crash near Point Barrow, Alaska. Following Rogers’ death, his planned appearance was removed from the project. The finished cartoon would nevertheless preserve the remarkable connection between Hollywood and polo that Rogers had helped popularize.

Walt Disney Brings Polo to Mickey Mouse

In 1936, Disney released Mickey’s Polo Team, bringing together two important parts of Walt Disney’s life: Hollywood and polo. The animated short depicts a chaotic and comedic polo match involving Mickey Mouse and other familiar Disney characters alongside caricatures of famous Hollywood personalities. Mickey captains one side of the match, while the cartoon incorporates characters including Donald Duck, Goofy and the Big Bad Wolf. The opposing Hollywood team includes recognizable celebrity caricatures, including Charlie Chaplin.

For today’s viewers, Mickey’s Polo Team is much more than an old Disney cartoon. It provides a fascinating glimpse into just how prominent polo was in American popular culture during the 1930s. Audiences could understand the joke of movie stars gathering for a polo match because real movie stars actually did gather around Southern California’s polo fields.

When Mickey Mouse Met the Sport of Kings

The combination was particularly appropriate.

Polo has long been known as “the Sport of Kings,” with a history stretching back centuries before the development of the modern game.

Mickey Mouse represented something entirely different: the rapidly expanding world of American popular entertainment. In Mickey’s Polo Team, those two worlds collided. The result created an unusual intersection of Disney history, Hollywood history and polo history. Nearly a century later, the cartoon also serves as a reminder of a period when polo occupied a much larger place in American popular culture than many people realize today.

Why Did Walt Disney Stop Playing Polo?

As Disney became increasingly confident on the polo field, he sought stronger competition and reportedly played with more accomplished players.

But polo was, and remains, a fast, physically demanding sport.

Players ride at considerable speed while maneuvering their horses, swinging long-handled mallets and competing for possession of the ball. Falls and collisions are inherent risks of the game. Disney eventually suffered injuries associated with playing polo and ultimately stopped participating in the sport. Although his playing career ended, his fascination with polo left behind an unusual chapter in Disney history.

It also left polo fans with something much more tangible: Mickey Mouse playing the Sport of Kings.

Mickey Mouse playing the Sport of Kings.

Walt Disney Wasn’t Hollywood’s Only Polo Player

Disney’s involvement in polo was part of a much larger Hollywood phenomenon. During polo’s golden age in Southern California, the sport attracted entertainers, actors, producers and celebrities who came to play, watch matches and socialize around the field.

Will Rogers was among its most enthusiastic participants, but he was hardly alone. The connection between Hollywood and polo is one reason vintage photographs, films, advertisements, programs and memorabilia from this era remain so fascinating. Disney may be one of the world’s most famous creative figures associated with the sport, but he was part of a much larger Hollywood polo community.

Walt Disney’s Polo Legacy

Walt Disney’s years playing polo reveal a side of the legendary animator that many Disney fans never encounter.

He wasn’t simply an animator who happened to enjoy horses. For a period of his life, Disney became genuinely enthusiastic about learning and playing polo. He studied the game, practiced his swing, played competitively and surrounded himself with other people who shared his enthusiasm.Eventually, he brought that passion into his animation studio.

The result was Mickey’s Polo Team. Nearly a century later, the short remains an entertaining reminder of a remarkable period when Walt Disney, Mickey Mouse, Will Rogers, Hollywood and the sport of polo all became part of the same story.
